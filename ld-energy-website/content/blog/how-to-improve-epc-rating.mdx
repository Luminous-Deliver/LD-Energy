The most cost-effective path to a better EPC rating generally starts with LED lighting and loft insulation, then cavity wall insulation where the property is suitable. Solid-wall properties are considerably more expensive to improve, because the cheap wall measure is not available to them.

## Why improving your EPC rating matters in 2026

An EPC rating affects three things directly: legal lettability, saleability, and energy bills. Under the [Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards (MEES)](/blog/mees-regulations-2026), rentals within scope must be rated E or have a valid registered exemption. The [confirmed higher standard for 1 October 2030](/blog/mees-timeline-2026-2030) still requires implementing legislation. See our [EPC for landlords guide](/blog/epc-for-landlords-2026) for the full compliance picture.

For sellers, the rating is one of the things buyers and their advisers look at, and a better rating is one less question to answer during a sale. See our [EPC for selling guide](/blog/epc-for-selling-house) for pre-sale strategy.

## What each measure does, and what decides its impact

| Measure | Why it can affect an EPC | What determines the impact |
|---|---|---|
| LED lighting | Lamp counts and types in permanent fittings are recorded | Existing LED, fluorescent/CFL and incandescent lamps |
| Heating controls | Controls affect calculated heating efficiency | What controls exist now, and the heating system type |
| Draught-proofing | Reduces uncontrolled heat loss | Existing air-tightness and where the gaps are |
| Hot-water cylinder insulation | Cylinder insulation type and thickness are recorded | Whether the cylinder is currently uninsulated or thinly insulated |
| Loft insulation | Reduces heat loss through the roof | Existing depth, roof construction, and whether it can be inspected |
| Cavity wall insulation | May reduce wall heat loss where the walls are suitable | Wall construction, whether the cavity is already filled, property geometry |
| Boiler replacement | Heating efficiency is a major input | The existing system, its age and type, and the fuel |
| Solid wall insulation | Addresses the largest heat-loss element in a solid-wall home | Wall area relative to the dwelling, and internal vs external approach |
| Solar PV | On-site generation offsets calculated energy use | System size, orientation, shading and roof area |
| Heat pump | Changes the heating system and its fuel entirely | What it replaces, the fuel it displaces and the dwelling fabric |

Measures are listed roughly cheapest-first. Actual installation costs vary materially by property, specification, access, installer and location, so get quotes from suitably qualified installers rather than working from a published range.

## D-to-C paths for common London property types

### Victorian and Edwardian terraces (pre-1919, solid walls)

The hardest property type to upgrade affordably, because solid brick walls cannot be cavity-filled and the wall is usually the largest heat-loss element.

**If the boiler is an older non-condensing type,** replacing it is usually the largest single lever available, because heating efficiency is a major input. Combined with loft insulation, heating controls and LED lighting, that is the cheapest package for this property type.

**If the boiler is already a modern condensing unit,** that lever is largely spent. What remains is the fabric — wall insulation — or on-site generation such as solar PV. Both are substantially more expensive, and external wall insulation may need planning consent, or be restricted entirely in a conservation area.

For inner-London Victorian stock in [Hackney](/areas/hackney), [Islington](/areas/islington), [Lambeth](/areas/lambeth) and [Wandsworth](/areas/wandsworth), the practical question is usually whether solar PV is viable, since it avoids touching protected elevations.

### 1930s–1970s semis (cavity walls)

Usually the most straightforward London properties to upgrade, because an unfilled cavity is comparatively cheap to insulate. Common in [Ealing](/areas/ealing), [Croydon](/areas/croydon), [Bromley](/areas/bromley), [Enfield](/areas/enfield) and [Barnet](/areas/barnet).

**The usual order of works:**
1. Cavity wall insulation, where the cavity is unfilled and suitable
2. Top up loft insulation
3. LED lighting throughout

This is normally the cheapest combination available to this property type, which is why these homes tend to have the shortest route to a higher band.

Funding for suitable insulation may be available, but check current eligibility and delivery capacity. GBIS has ended. See our [cavity wall insulation guide](/blog/cavity-wall-insulation-epc) and [loft insulation guide](/blog/loft-insulation-epc-rating) for details.

### Post-war flats (1945–1980, [Tower Hamlets](/areas/tower-hamlets), [Newham](/areas/newham))

Purpose-built flats from this era often have concrete construction and communal or district heating. The EPC depends heavily on the heating system:

- **Electric storage heaters:** typically D or E, replacing with a gas combi or modern heat pump is the highest-impact single measure
- **Communal gas:** often C already; individual leaseholders have limited room for improvement
- **District heating:** varies enormously by scheme vintage

For leaseholders, speak to your freeholder about communal system upgrades and check what individual measures, glazing upgrades, cylinder insulation, are within your control.

## Free and subsidised schemes in 2026

### ECO4 (Energy Company Obligation)

ECO4 funds insulation and heating upgrades for eligible households:
- Households receiving means-tested benefits (Universal Credit, Pension Credit, Housing Benefit)
- Private tenants or owner-occupiers in D–G-rated properties
- Some local authority referral routes (check your borough council)

GBIS ended on 31 March 2026. ECO4 runs until 31 December 2026 to complete existing obligations and remediation. Check eligibility and actual funding availability with a supplier or local authority before committing to work.

### Great British Insulation Scheme (GBIS)

GBIS ended on 31 March 2026. It is not a current application route.

### Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS)

The scheme offers £7,500 towards an eligible air-to-water heat pump. EPC C is not a general eligibility requirement. Check the [current GOV.UK eligibility rules](https://www.gov.uk/apply-boiler-upgrade-scheme/check-if-youre-eligible) and speak to an MCS-certified installer about suitability and the application.

## What the EPC calculation considers

The software models the whole dwelling: construction and insulation, dimensions, heating and controls, hot water, ventilation, glazing, renewables and lighting. There is no universal ranking that predicts the effect of an improvement for every property.

Cosmetic decoration does not itself improve the recorded energy features. For energy upgrades, use the property-specific assessment and suitable installation advice.

## Getting a new EPC after improvements

Improvements do not automatically invalidate an existing EPC. A new assessment can record completed work when there is a reason to update the rating. Keep supporting evidence; the new result is not guaranteed to be higher.
