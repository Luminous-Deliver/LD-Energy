Many London flats, and some houses, are heated entirely by electricity: storage heaters, panel heaters, an electric boiler or a heat pump. Electric heating is one of the most common reasons a home gets a lower rating than its owner expects. This guide explains why, and what the assessor needs to record your system correctly.

## Why electric heating often rates lower

The headline EPC rating is based on modelled running costs, not on how warm the home feels or how much carbon it emits. The method works those costs out using a fixed table of fuel prices in the RdSAP 10 specification:

| Fuel | Price per kWh in the method |
|---|---|
| Mains gas | 3.48p |
| Electricity, standard tariff | 13.19p |
| Electricity, 7-hour tariff, high rate | 15.29p |
| Electricity, 7-hour tariff, low rate | 5.50p |

These are fixed prices used for every EPC so that homes can be compared. They are not today's bills. They do explain the pattern: a unit of standard-rate electricity costs nearly four times as much as a unit of gas in the calculation, so the same home usually rates lower heated by direct electricity than by a gas boiler.

## Your meter matters

Storage heaters charge overnight on cheaper off-peak electricity, so the method costs them mostly at the low rate. That only works if the home has a meter that records two rates.

- **Storage heaters with a single-rate meter** are recorded as panel heaters, costed at the standard rate.
- **Two separate meters**, one for on-peak and one for off-peak, are recorded as a dual-rate meter.
- **A time-switch** is treated as dual rate. **A tele-switch** is treated as single rate.
- **A smart meter** can be used as evidence if the unit or its display shows the readings. It does not need to be working at the visit.

The assessor may need to see a recent electricity bill to confirm your tariff. Have one ready, on paper or in your supplier's app.

## How each electric system is recorded

### Storage heaters

The assessor records the type: old large-volume, fan-assisted, integrated storage and direct-acting, or high heat retention. A high heat retention storage heater is only recorded as one if its brand and model are in the product database. If the assessor cannot find it there, it is recorded as a fan-assisted storage heater. The make and model are usually on a label on the side or underneath, or in the manual.

If you have more than one type of storage heater, they are treated as two main heating systems. A storage heater in a hallway or another room that is not a habitable room still counts as the main heating if it is the only heating in the home.

### Panel heaters, electric radiators, underfloor mats and infra-red heaters

Direct electric heaters that heat straight from the mains are recorded as electric panel heaters. That includes electric underfloor heating mats and infra-red heaters.

### Electric boilers

An electric combi boiler is recorded as a direct-acting electric boiler, with fuel type electricity.

### Heat pumps

A heat pump is recorded from the product database by make and model. If it is not found there, a default efficiency is used. The system's design flow temperature is recorded as unknown unless documents show it was designed and commissioned as a low temperature system. Keep the installation and commissioning certificates, and show them to the assessor.

## What to have ready

- A recent electricity bill showing your tariff.
- Access to the meter, or meters, and the consumer unit.
- The make and model of storage heaters or a heat pump, or their manuals.
- Installation and commissioning paperwork for a heat pump.
- Access to the hot water cylinder and immersion heater, if there is one.

## Improving the rating of an electrically heated home

The suggested improvements on your EPC show the measures modelled for your home. For electrically heated homes they often include heating controls, insulation and, where suitable, a change of heating system. What each one is worth depends on the whole home, so treat any figure as specific to that certificate.

If you are thinking of changing the heating, get advice from a suitably qualified installer first.
