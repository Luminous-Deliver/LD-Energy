The current domestic Minimum Energy Efficiency Standard is **EPC E** for properties within scope in England and Wales, unless a valid exemption is registered. EPC assessment identifies the rating; commissioning an assessment does not itself make a property compliant.

## What MEES currently requires

MEES applies to specified domestic tenancy types where the property is legally required to have an EPC. For those properties, the restriction on letting below E began for new tenancies in April 2018 and extended to continuing tenancies on 1 April 2020.

- An F- or G-rated property within scope must be improved to E or have a valid registered exemption before it is let or continues to be let.
- A vacant property that is not being offered for letting does not require immediate improvement solely under MEES.
- EPC provision duties are separate. An EPC generally must be commissioned before marketing, with reasonable efforts to obtain it within seven days and a further 21 days allowed if those efforts fail.
- An HMO, holiday let or protected building needs an individual scope check. None is automatically covered or exempt just because of its label.

## Penalties for non-compliance

Local authorities enforce domestic MEES. These are the **current maximum financial penalties**, not automatic charges:

| Breach | Current maximum |
|---|---|
| Letting below the standard for under three months | £2,000 |
| Letting below the standard for three months or more | £4,000 |
| False or misleading information on the exemption register | £1,000 |
| Failure to comply with a compliance notice | £2,000 |
| Total financial penalty per property | £5,000 |

A publication penalty may also apply. Government plans a £30,000 maximum per property per breach under the future standard, but this still needs legislation. Do not use that future figure to calculate today's liability.

Respond to a compliance notice within its stated time limits. A plan to improve later is not itself a registered exemption or permission to keep letting below E.

## Exemptions: evidence and registration

The following describes the current E standard. An exemption only applies if its conditions are met and it is properly registered. Most last five years; check the specific expiry and ownership rules.

### All relevant improvements made

If all relevant improvements possible within the **£3,500 including VAT** cap have been made but the property remains below E, this exemption may apply. Retain the relevant report, installation details and cost evidence. It can also apply where no relevant improvements can be made.

### High-cost exemption

This is a different route: even the cheapest recommended measure would exceed £3,500 including VAT. GOV.UK requires three installer quotations and the specified supporting confirmation. Adding up a wish list of expensive works is not sufficient.

### Third-party consent

Consent may be needed from a tenant, superior landlord, lender or planning authority. Document reasonable efforts to obtain it and the response. Tenant refusal is not a blanket exemption from every measure; a tenant-consent exemption ends when that tenancy ends.

### Devaluation and wall insulation

A devaluation exemption requires the specified independent RICS surveyor's evidence that relevant improvements would reduce the property's value by more than 5%. A separate wall-insulation exemption requires appropriate expert evidence of harm to the building's fabric or structure.

### Listed buildings and new landlords

Listed status is not an automatic exemption. First check whether the building is exempt from the EPC requirement because compliance would unacceptably alter its character or appearance. If an EPC and MEES apply, assess the relevant MEES exemption separately.

Certain circumstances of becoming a landlord can qualify for a six-month temporary exemption. An ordinary purchase does not automatically qualify. Check the official list before relying on it.

## Common property situations

**An F-rated property with an existing tenant:** check scope and any registered exemption promptly. If covered and not exempt, the continuing letting is also subject to MEES; waiting for the next tenant does not resolve it.

**A solid-wall terrace in [Islington](/areas/islington) or [Hackney](/areas/hackney):** use the property's recommendation report and installation advice. A building's age or starting band cannot establish which package will reach E or C, or its cost.

**An auction purchase:** establish the EPC, tenancy, exemption and improvement position before committing. Do not assume a six-month grace period or a standard improvement budget.

## Confirmed future policy: 1 October 2030

The January 2026 government response confirmed a **single compliance date of 1 October 2030** for tenancies in scope. It replaces the earlier phased proposal. The higher standard uses fabric performance plus either heating-system performance or smart readiness under reformed EPC metrics.

The confirmed policy includes a **£10,000 cost cap** and a transition for homes with an existing Energy Efficiency Rating of C or above before 1 October 2029, lasting until that EPC expires or is replaced. These are future policy decisions requiring implementing legislation, not today's E-standard rules.

## Practical next steps

1. Check the current EPC and whether the tenancy falls within scope.
2. For an F or G, review the standard recommendations and obtain relevant installation advice and quotes.
3. Check exemption requirements and register only with the required evidence.
4. Confirm funding availability. GBIS ended on 31 March 2026; ECO4 runs until 31 December 2026 for existing obligations and remediation. Funding is not guaranteed.
