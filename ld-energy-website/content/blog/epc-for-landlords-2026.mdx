Privately rented property covered by the MEES Regulations must have a valid EPC rated band E or above, unless a valid exemption is registered. That has applied to all tenancies, not just new ones, since April 2020, and the maximum penalty is currently £5,000 per property in total.

Government has confirmed a higher standard equivalent to EPC C for 1 October 2030, using reformed EPC metrics. Implementing legislation is still required. The current E minimum remains in force for rentals within MEES scope, unless a valid exemption is registered. For how the current position compares with the confirmed future policy, see our [MEES timeline for landlords](/blog/mees-timeline-2026-2030).

## Current legal requirements at a glance

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Minimum EPC band | E (SAP 39+) |
| In force since | April 2020 (all tenancies) |
| Letting below standard for under three months | Up to £2,000 |
| Letting below standard for three months or more | Up to £4,000 |
| Maximum fine | £5,000 per property in total (£30,000 proposed, not yet law) |
| EPC validity | 10 years from issue |
| Exemption register | PRS Exemptions Register (gov.uk) |
| Confirmed future policy | Higher standard equivalent to C from 1 October 2030, using reformed metrics; legislation required |

The obligation attaches to the **act of letting**, granting, renewing, or continuing a tenancy. A statutory periodic tenancy (rolling over after a fixed term) is treated as a continuation of the existing tenancy for MEES purposes.

Local authorities enforce MEES and may publish breaches as well as impose financial penalties. Check the [official current guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/domestic-private-rented-property-minimum-energy-efficiency-standard-landlord-guidance) for the detailed limits and review process.

## Step 1, Know your portfolio's current ratings

Check the government EPC register (find-energy-certificate.service.gov.uk) for every property. Flags requiring immediate action:

- **Rating F or G:** If within MEES scope and without a valid exemption, the property cannot be let or continue to be let below E.
- **Expiring certificate:** Check when a replacement is legally required. Expiry during an unchanged continuing tenancy is not by itself a universal requirement to renew immediately.
- **No EPC on register:** Commission an assessment before the next letting (common for pre-2008 properties).
- **Rating D or lower:** Worth planning improvements against the confirmed higher standard for 1 October 2030, subject to legislation.

## Step 2, Understand your property types

Improvement costs and feasibility vary enormously by construction. Categorise your portfolio:

### Cavity-wall properties (1920–1990)

Found across outer London, [Ealing](/areas/ealing), [Croydon](/areas/croydon), [Bromley](/areas/bromley), [Enfield](/areas/enfield), [Barnet](/areas/barnet), Sutton, Havering, and Bexley. Measures to investigate where suitable include:

- Cavity wall insulation, where the cavity is unfilled and suitable
- Topping up loft insulation
- LED lighting throughout

These are the cheaper measures, and generally the right order to do them in. What they are worth on a specific property is calculated from the whole dwelling, and appears on that property's EPC recommendation report.
There is no standard budget that guarantees C from D. Obtain property-specific assessment and installer quotes.

GBIS ended on 31 March 2026. ECO4 runs until 31 December 2026 to complete existing obligations and remediation; eligibility and actual availability must be checked with a supplier or local authority.

### Victorian and Edwardian solid-wall properties (pre-1919)

The dominant type in inner London, [Hackney](/areas/hackney), [Islington](/areas/islington), [Lambeth](/areas/lambeth), [Wandsworth](/areas/wandsworth), Southwark, [Tower Hamlets](/areas/tower-hamlets), [Newham](/areas/newham). Possible interventions need a property-specific suitability check:

- External wall insulation: check moisture risks, permissions and conservation constraints
- Internal wall insulation: check detailing, ventilation and loss of usable floor space
- Solar PV: assess roof suitability, shading, permissions and electrical design
- Lighting, loft insulation and controls: review what is already present

Get the property assessed before choosing measures. The starting score alone cannot identify a reliable improvement package. [Our domestic EPC assessment](/services/domestic-epc) has guide prices from £65.

### Post-war flats and maisonettes (1945–1980)

Common in [Tower Hamlets](/areas/tower-hamlets), [Lambeth](/areas/lambeth), Southwark, [Newham](/areas/newham), [Hackney](/areas/hackney), and Brent. The heating system is often the critical factor:

- Electric storage heaters: record the actual model and controls
- Communal heat network: obtain available system information
- Individual boiler: review the model, controls and building fabric

A heating change requires property-specific design advice and may need freeholder consent. Do not assume a particular system will deliver the largest EPC improvement.

## Step 3, Prioritise improvements strategically

**Priority order:**

1. **F/G properties first**, check MEES scope and any valid registered exemption; act promptly where required
2. **E properties next**, plan for the proposed C deadline; consider access and disruption when scheduling work
3. **D properties third** — if band C is legislated, D becomes the compliance risk
4. **Use ECO4 wherever eligible**, the scheme is time-limited; apply now for any tenant receiving means-tested benefits
5. **Batch by property type**, ask assessors and installers about feasible scheduling; capacity is confirmed individually

## Step 4, Exemptions: when and how to use them

Exemptions are narrow and must be registered on the PRS Exemptions Register **before** you continue letting. An unregistered exemption is not valid.

**Cost-related exemptions:** The current cap is £3,500 including VAT. The all-improvements-made exemption applies when relevant work within the cap is completed but the property remains below E. The separate high-cost exemption needs evidence that even the cheapest recommended measure exceeds the cap, including three installer quotes. The confirmed future cap is £10,000, subject to legislation.

**Consent exemption:** A tenant, lender, or planning authority has refused consent in writing. Document everything.

**Devaluation exemption:** An independent surveyor confirms the works would reduce market value by more than 5%.

**Listed buildings:** Listed status is not an automatic exemption. Establish whether an EPC is required and, if MEES applies, whether a specific registered exemption is available.

Common mistakes: getting only one quote, not completing sub-cap measures before claiming the exemption, letting the five-year exemption lapse without re-registering.

## Step 5, The proposed C standard: planning now

The C standard is not yet law, but the 2030 planning horizon is worth working to. Key actions:

1. **Know your scores**, commission assessments for any property without a current EPC
2. **Model improvement costs per property type**, obtain suitable advice and installer quotes for each building
3. **Check ECO4 eligibility** — scheme rules and end dates change, so confirm current eligibility on GOV.UK
4. **Schedule works between tenancies**, insulation and wall works in an empty property are faster and cheaper
5. **Factor into purchase decisions**, investigate actual works and costs before buying; no budget follows from a band alone
