An EPC is a one-off cost that lasts 10 years. What you pay depends on the size of the home and how quickly you need the certificate. Who pays depends on whether the home is being sold or let.

## What sets the price

The main factor is the home's internal floor area. A bigger home takes longer to measure and record, so the price rises in bands of floor area. The price table on this page shows each band, from a studio to a large house.

We charge the same in every London borough, with no travel charges and no VAT to add. If you do not know the floor area, choose "Not sure of floor area" on the quote form. Either way, we confirm your exact price before you book.

## What is included

- **The visit.** An accredited assessor measures and records the home. The visit takes 45 to 90 minutes, depending on its size.
- **The certificate.** We lodge the EPC on the government register within 72 hours of the visit as standard.
- **Suggested improvements.** The EPC itself lists suggested improvements for the home, unless there is no reasonable potential for any.

A floor plan measured on the same visit costs less than booking the two separately. The [floor plans service](/services/floor-plans) and [pricing page](/pricing) show both.

## Who pays when you sell

The seller. The regulations put the duty to commission the EPC on the seller, before the home goes on the market, and the seller must make it available to buyers free of charge. If your estate agent arranges the EPC for you, the duty is still yours, so ask the agent what they charge for it.

## Who pays when you let

The landlord. The landlord must commission the EPC before marketing the home and give the tenant a copy free of charge.

A landlord or letting agent in England cannot pass that cost on. Under the Tenant Fees Act 2019, any payment a landlord or agent requires from a tenant is prohibited unless the Act permits it. The permitted payments are rent, a tenancy deposit, a holding deposit, certain default fees, payments to change or end a tenancy early at the tenant's request, and bills such as council tax, utilities, a TV licence and communication services. An EPC is not among them.

A tenant living in the home must let the landlord's assessor in for the visit. Our [guide for landlords](/blog/epc-for-landlords-2026) covers the minimum rating a rented home needs.

## Do you need to pay at all?

Maybe not. An EPC is valid for 10 years and belongs to the home, so a certificate lodged for a previous owner or tenancy may still be in date. Search the [GOV.UK find an energy certificate service](https://www.gov.uk/find-energy-certificate) by postcode before you book.

You may still want a new one if the home has changed. New insulation, a new boiler or replacement windows are only recorded by a new assessment. Our guide on [how long an EPC lasts](/blog/how-long-does-epc-last) covers when you need a new certificate.
