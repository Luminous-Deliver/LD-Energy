An EPC generally must be commissioned before marketing a home for sale in England and Wales. Reasonable efforts must be made to obtain it within seven days, with a further 21 days allowed if those efforts fail. Exemptions apply. The EPC must also be made available to prospective buyers as required and given to the eventual buyer.

## The legal position for sellers

The Energy Performance of Buildings Regulations set commissioning and provision duties, subject to exemptions. Specifically:

- **Before marketing:** The EPC must be commissioned before the property is advertised in any medium, portal, window card, social media
- **In all advertising:** Where an EPC is available, commercial advertising must include the energy performance indicator; a full coloured chart is not universally required
- **On request:** The full certificate must be provided free of charge to any prospective buyer who asks for it
- **At completion:** The EPC must be handed to the buyer

The seller's penalty for failing to provide an EPC is a **£200 fixed penalty**. An agent must check that the EPC has been commissioned and meet the applicable duties. Ask your agent how this fits their listing process.

**Exemptions:** Some standalone buildings under 50m² and qualifying protected buildings are exempt; listed status alone is not enough. A property that will be demolished immediately on sale may also be exempt.

## When to commission the EPC

**Before you formally instruct an agent.** Many sellers lose time by assuming the EPC happens during conveyancing, it must precede any marketing activity.

Practical timeline for a London sale:

| Timeframe | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| 4–5 weeks before listing | Decide on agent; plan property preparation |
| 3–4 weeks before listing | Commission EPC assessment |
| 2–3 weeks before listing | Certificate received; share with agent for listing prep |
| Day of listing | Marketing goes live with EPC rating in all ads |

For urgent situations, next-day lodgement is available across all London boroughs for an additional £15.

## Does EPC rating affect sale price?

An EPC gives buyers information about the assessed building and modelled energy performance. It does not predict the sale price, actual household bills or the return on an improvement. Ask your selling agent about the local market and obtain installation quotes before spending money solely to influence a sale.

For a buyer intending to let, [current MEES scope and exemptions](/blog/mees-regulations-2026) also matter. A higher rating does not remove every landlord obligation.

## What rating to aim for before listing

### Currently D-rated

Check the exact score and the standard recommendation report. Being close to C does not prove that a particular low-cost measure will cross the boundary. Review the assessed property, suitability and installation quotes before choosing work.

### Currently E-rated

The report can help identify possible improvements. No fixed budget or package can guarantee D or C, and a higher score does not guarantee a higher sale price. Useful starting points include our [low-cost improvement guide](/blog/cheapest-ways-improve-epc) and [full improvement guide](/blog/how-to-improve-epc-rating).

### Currently F or G-rated

There is no general minimum EPC rating for a home to be sold. A buyer planning to rent it out must check the applicable MEES requirements. Discuss the assessed property and possible improvements rather than assuming a standard route to E.

## Getting the EPC before or after improvements

**Option A, EPC first, then improve, then re-assess**

Get the EPC to confirm your exact SAP score. Then make targeted improvements. Then commission a second assessment to confirm the new rating. Cost: two assessment fees (guide prices from £65 each). Advantage: decisions can use the assessment and its modelled recommendations. The eventual rating is not guaranteed.

**Option B, Improve, then get one EPC**

Where suitable work is already planned, an assessment after completion can record the finished property. Keep supporting evidence. Do not delay commissioning an EPC that is legally required while waiting for works.

Option A is lower-risk and the approach we recommend for sellers unsure about their current score.
