Improving an EPC rating does not have to mean a heat pump and a five-figure bill. Most homes climb the scale through a series of modest, well-chosen measures, and the right ones depend on your budget and what the property already has. This guide starts with the cheapest measures, then covers what bigger budgets buy.

The five cheapest improvements — LED lighting, draught-proofing, a hot water cylinder jacket, heating controls and loft insulation — are inexpensive relative to fabric or heating work, potentially useful where suitable. Their effect depends on what is already installed and the assessment methodology; they do not guarantee a move to another band.

## Why cheap improvements matter

Most of the marketing attention around EPC improvements focuses on heat pumps, external wall insulation, and solar panels, larger projects that cost thousands of pounds rather than hundreds. They matter for reaching band B or A. For a home moving from E to D, or D to C, the cheap measures are where to start.

Review low-cost measures where suitable, using the actual assessment. Their combined effect cannot be inferred from a budget or a fixed list of point gains.

## The five best improvements under £500

### 1. LED lighting throughout

RdSAP 10 records lamp counts and types in permanent fittings, distinguishing LED, fluorescent/CFL and incandescent lamps. A fitting with several lamps contributes several lamps to the count.

The effect of replacing older lamps depends on what is already installed and the whole-property calculation. Plug-in table lamps are not part of this count. See the [current lighting conventions](/guides/rdsap-10-what-changed-2025) for the assessment rules. The effect is largest in a house that still has mostly old bulbs and negligible in one already fully converted.

### 2. Loft insulation to 270mm

If you have an accessible pitched loft with less than 270mm of insulation, topping it up is the highest-impact of the cheap measures — and may qualify for funding, subject to current availability.

The assessment credits insulation depth, measured at the time of the visit. Ensure the loft is accessible for the assessor and the insulation is freshly laid and at depth. Our guide to [loft insulation and EPC ratings](/guides/loft-insulation-epc-rating) has the details.

**Funding:** GBIS ended on 31 March 2026. ECO4 ends on 31 December 2026, and funded work has to be finished by then. Check eligibility and actual funding availability with a supplier or local authority before committing to work.

### 3. Draught-proofing

Draught-proofing can reduce uncontrolled air leakage; an EPC visit is not an air-tightness test. Gaps around windows, doors, skirting boards, loft hatches, and pipework all allow cold air in and warm air out.

RdSAP 10 includes conventions for recording window and door draught-proofing. This does not give every draught-proofing measure a fixed point value. Preserve intentional ventilation and seek advice before blocking any opening.

**What to tackle:**
- Loft hatch: fit an insulated board cover
- External doors: fit brush or rubber seal strips
- Sash windows: fit brush strip around the frame
- Chimney (if unused): fit a chimney balloon or draught excluder
- Letterbox: fit an insulating cover

Draught-proofing is largely DIY-able, which is what keeps it cheap; an installer costs more but covers the whole property properly.

### 4. Hot water cylinder jacket

If your property has a separate hot water storage cylinder (rather than a combi boiler which heats water on demand), a well-insulated jacket dramatically reduces standing heat loss.

An uninsulated cylinder loses useful heat continuously to the surrounding air. RdSAP records cylinder insulation type and thickness separately, so an adequately insulated cylinder is credited where a bare one is not.

**Check:**
- Does your cylinder have a jacket? (A white or silver foil-wrapped jacket around the outside)
- Is it 80mm thick? (Check by feel, a thin jacket is 25mm, a proper one is 80mm+)

If it is uninsulated or thinly insulated, a jacket is inexpensive, widely available and takes about half an hour to fit. Pound for pound it is the best-value item on this list. If the cylinder is already factory-insulated foam, or you have a combi boiler with no cylinder, this does not apply.

### 5. Heating controls

RdSAP 10 separately credits three control types:
- **Room thermostat:** the main heating is controlled by a thermostat in the living area, not just a timer.
- **Programmer (timer):** heating runs on a timed programme rather than manual-on-permanent.
- **Thermostatic radiator valves (TRVs):** each radiator can be controlled individually.

Each is recorded separately, so a property missing all three has more to gain than one that already has a thermostat. Many older systems are missing the room thermostat or TRVs.

Ask a heating engineer what controls are suitable for the existing system; the EPC effect depends on the system and approved calculation.

A smart-control brand alone does not establish its EPC treatment. The assessor records the identifiable control functions and applicable product information under the methodology.

**Installation:**
- Smart thermostat + programmer: DIY, or fitted by a heating engineer
- TRVs (replacing manual valves): inexpensive per radiator as a DIY job, or a modest plumber cost for the whole house

## Combining the five measures

These are options to investigate, not a universal order of works. The sensible sequence depends on suitability, existing systems and installation quotes.

What that sequence is actually worth on your property is calculated from the whole dwelling, and the suggested improvements on your EPC show an indicative rating after the recommended measures for that specific home.

The starting band or score cannot show whether lighting and loft insulation will be enough to reach C. Use the actual assessment and suitable installation advice.

Funding can change the budget, but confirm eligibility, availability and installation quotes before relying on it.

## What can you do with a bigger budget?

Above £500, the options move to wall insulation, then the heating system and bigger projects. The tiers below are rough budgets, not quotes, and none promises a point gain or a move to C. With a couple of thousand pounds you can do everything above and add cavity wall insulation. With several thousand the heating system and generation come into range.

### A couple of thousand pounds: cavity wall insulation

Where the property has unfilled cavity walls, filling them is usually the largest gain in this price range. Most homes built between roughly 1930 and 1990 have cavities, and many remain unfilled. A borescope check confirms suitability, and exposure to driving rain needs assessing first. The cost can pass £500 depending on wall area and access, and it may be eligible for funding while schemes are open. Cavity-walled homes generally have a cheaper route to a higher band than solid-walled ones. Our [cavity wall insulation guide](/guides/cavity-wall-insulation-epc) covers when it is and is not a good idea.

A common London scenario, typical of the inter-war and 1950s semis across [Ealing](/areas/ealing), [Croydon](/areas/croydon) and [Enfield](/areas/enfield): a mid-terrace rated mid-D, with unfilled cavities, 100mm of loft insulation, partial LED lighting and no TRVs. These measures may be worth investigating, but this is an illustration, not a verified case study or a prediction of cost or rating. The specific geometry, heating and installed insulation must be assessed.

### Several thousand pounds: heating and bigger projects

**Replacing an old non-condensing boiler.** If your boiler is an older non-condensing unit, replacing it with a modern condensing boiler is the standout measure in this tier, because heating efficiency is a major input to the calculation and heating usually dominates a home's energy use. If your boiler is already a condensing model, replacing it changes little, so spend the money elsewhere.

**Solar PV, partial install.** A smaller solar array fits this budget and helps by generating electricity on site. How much depends on roof orientation, shading and array size. Solar is also one of the few measures that keeps helping beyond band C, which matters if you are aiming for B. Full systems cost thousands of pounds, and suitability and EPC effect depend on the property. Our guide to [solar panels and your EPC rating](/guides/solar-panels-epc-rating) works through how many points different systems add.

**Secondary glazing.** Where full double glazing is impractical or not permitted, for example in conservation areas, secondary glazing improves the modelled window performance and cuts draughts and noise. The effect on the rating is usually modest, so treat it as a comfort and compliance measure rather than a route to a new band. New double glazing is expensive and changes little where the property already has it.

## How do the measures compare?

Measures roughly cheapest-first. What each one does, and what decides how much it helps your particular property:

| Measure | Why it can affect an EPC | What determines the impact |
|---|---|---|
| LED lighting | Lamp counts and types in permanent fittings are recorded | Existing LED, fluorescent/CFL and incandescent lamps |
| Hot water cylinder jacket | Cylinder insulation type and thickness are recorded | Whether the cylinder is bare, thinly insulated or already foam-insulated |
| Heating controls | Controls affect calculated heating efficiency | Which of thermostat, programmer and TRVs already exist |
| Draught proofing | Reduces uncontrolled heat loss | Existing air-tightness and where the gaps are |
| Loft insulation top-up | Reduces heat loss through the roof | Existing depth, roof construction, whether it can be inspected |
| Cavity wall insulation | May reduce wall heat loss where walls are suitable | Wall construction, whether the cavity is already filled, geometry |
| Condensing boiler | Heating efficiency is a major input | The existing system, its age and type, and the fuel |
| Secondary glazing | Improves modelled window performance | Existing glazing, and how much of the property is covered |

Installation costs vary materially by property, specification, access, installer and location, so get quotes from suitably qualified installers. We carry out the EPC assessment. We do not quote for or install this work.

## What should you keep in mind before budgeting?

**Gains depend on your starting point.** The same measure can matter a great deal in an uninsulated 1930s semi and barely register in a 1990s flat. The lower your starting rating, the bigger the gains tend to be, because the model rewards fixing the worst problems first.

**Points are not simply additive.** RdSAP models the whole dwelling, so measures interact. Insulating the walls reduces heating demand, which in turn shrinks the benefit of a more efficient boiler. Two measures will usually deliver less together than they would apart. If you need to hit a specific band, ask an assessor to model the combination rather than adding up estimates.

**Check current funding.** See the [official scheme update](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/extending-the-eco4-end-date/outcome/extending-the-eco4-end-date-government-response-html) for ECO4 and GBIS.

**Recording a changed property.** Improvements do not automatically invalidate an existing EPC. A fresh assessment can record the completed work and establish the resulting rating; no particular outcome is promised.

## Where should you start?

Start with the certificate, not the chequebook. Your current EPC's recommendations section, or a fresh assessment if the certificate is old, tells you which of these measures apply to your property. Then work up the budget in order: the cheap measures first, wall insulation second, heating last.
