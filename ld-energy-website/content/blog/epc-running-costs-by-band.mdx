Every EPC estimates what the home costs a year to heat, to provide hot water and to light. We took those estimates from {cost:total} certificates lodged for existing London homes from {cost:from} to {cost:to}, and grouped them by rating.

## Running costs by band

<RunningCostTable />

"Typical" is the median: half the homes in the band cost less and half cost more. The middle half is the range from the lower to the upper quartile. Band A is left out because only a handful of certificates were lodged, and F and G are combined for the same reason.

## Flats, houses and what the money goes on

<RunningCostTable view="types" />

Flats cost less to run than houses in every band. They are smaller, and a flat with homes above, below and beside it loses less heat through its walls, floor and roof. Heating is the largest part of the cost in every band, and it is the figure that rises most from one band to the next.

For your own home, the EPC shows the estimated cost now and what its suggested improvements could save. Our guide on [how to improve your EPC rating](/blog/how-to-improve-epc-rating) covers the measures.

## What the figures include

- **Heating, hot water and lighting only.** Appliances, cooking and other electricity use are not included.
- **Standard use.** The method assumes standard heating hours and a number of occupants worked out from the floor area, not how your household uses the home.
- **The prices used for EPCs.** The fuel prices behind certificate costs are updated from time to time, and the costs on certificates move when they are. In the register data the latest change came on {cost:from}, so only certificates lodged from then are pooled here.
- **Existing homes in London.** New-build certificates are left out, and a home lodged twice in the period counts twice.

The rating itself works from the same kind of cost estimate, at a fixed set of prices written into the method. [EPC ratings explained](/blog/epc-ratings-explained) covers how the bands are set.
